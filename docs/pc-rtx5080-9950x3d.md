# Running on an RTX 5080, Ryzen 9 9950X3D and 32 GB

What to expect, what to set in Windows, and how to start it on this PC. Nothing here
has been measured on this PC yet: the figures below are this fork's, from the RX 7900 XTX
machine (`docs/findings.md`), and from fxceless's RTX 4070 machine
(`docs/README-fxceless.md`).

## What to expect

- **A stable 30 fps is not where this fork is on any PC yet.** The best measured is about
  53 ms a frame (about 19 fps) in a city street, on an i9-12900K and RX 7900 XTX with both
  renderer patches on.
- The wall is one CPU thread of ours: the one that prepares submissions, 48 ms of a 55 ms
  frame. The 9950X3D's cores are faster than the 12900K's and its 3D V-Cache suits that
  thread's work (compares and hashes over large buffers), but how much faster it is here
  has not been measured.
- The card was busy for about 41 ms of a frame on the 7900 XTX. On NVIDIA the layer takes
  other paths (waves of 32 lanes, not 64; and where the driver caps view heaps at
  1,000,000 views, as the code's comments say NVIDIA's did, no raw twins and fewer slots
  for tables made per draw: the heap size is probed at start). Nobody has measured an RTX card since
  this fork's speed work. The last NVIDIA figure (RTX 4070, fxceless) is from before it:
  2.5 to 4 fps.
- **32 GB of RAM is less than what the layer uses now**: 35 GB of private memory in the
  README, 40 to 46 GB in `docs/findings.md`. What does not fit goes to the page file, and
  that shows as stutter. 64 GB is the fix; until then, see the checklist.

## Before the first run

1. **Page file**: on the fastest NVMe drive, system managed, or at least 32 GB.
   Without enough room the layer's allocations fail.
2. **Close what you do not need**: the browser and launchers in particular.
   Every GB counts at 32 GB.
3. **EXPO** on in the BIOS (the DDR5 at its rated speed).
4. **AMD chipset driver** installed (it carries the 3D V-Cache driver), **Game Mode** on.
   In the Xbox Game Bar (Win+G) with the title running, tick "Remember this is a game"
   for `victory.exe`. Windows then prefers the V-Cache CCD for it.
5. **NVIDIA Control Panel**, program settings for `victory.exe`:
   - Power management mode: *Prefer maximum performance*.
   - Shader Cache Size: *Unlimited*. Pipelines the driver makes are the known cause of
     frames over 200 ms.
   - Max Frame Rate: 30, if you would rather have even frames at 30 once the layer can do
     more than that. It does nothing while the layer runs under 30.
6. **Ray tracing off**: it is off unless you pass `-RayTracing`, which costs about a third.
7. Translate the shaders, and make the pipelines ahead (README, "How to run it"):

   ```powershell
   target\diag\victory.exe shaders <the shader archives>
   target\diag\victory.exe warm --used
   ```

   Run `warm --used` again after a driver update or a new build.

## Starting it

```powershell
scripts\play-rtx5080.ps1 -Exe <path of your copy's executable>
```

This is `scripts\play.ps1` (all of its settings, unchanged) with:

| What | Why |
|---|---|
| `VICTORY_PORT_CONSTANTS=1` | Renderer patch 1. The README's 53 ms is with it on. It falls back to the old copy per region where it cannot apply (`crates/victory-gpu/src/port.rs`). |
| `VICTORY_PORT_DESCRIPTORS=1` | Renderer patch 2. Also part of that 53 ms. Every frame a check looks at pages the write watch saw written, and makes what the patches missed (`crates/victory-gpu/src/port_descriptors.rs`). |

Options:

- `-NoPatches` leaves both patches out. Try it first if the picture is wrong or the device is
  lost: both patches are off by default upstream while they are tested, and neither has
  been tried on NVIDIA.
- `-VCacheCcd` keeps the whole process on logical processors 0 to 15, which is CCD 0, the
  one with the V-Cache on a 9950X3D. It is the same idea as step 4, done without Windows'
  help. It also halves the threads the process may use (16 of 32), and the layer runs many
  threads of its own beside the title's. Whether it is faster here has to be measured.
- `-Safe`, `-RayTracing`, `-Build`, `-More` work as in `play.ps1`. `-Safe` also leaves the
  patches out.

Things this PC needs nothing for, because the layer already reads them from the hardware:

- **Card memory**: the budgets for copies in the card's memory and for textures follow the
  budget the 5080 reports, with a rule of their own for 16 GB cards
  (`crates/victory-gpu/src/shadow.rs`, `default_card_limits_mb`).
- **Wave size, view heap size, indirect dispatch size**: asked from the card at start
  (`crates/victory-gpu/src/host.rs`, `crates/victory-gpu/src/descriptor/host.rs`,
  `crates/victory-gpu/src/indirect.rs`).
- **The console's AMD-only CPU instructions** run natively on a Ryzen; any the CPU lacks
  are emulated when they fault, as on Intel (`crates/victory-loader/src/crash.rs`).

## Measuring

F3 shows the panel of numbers while the window has the focus. Compare runs in the
same place for a minute or more: scenes differ from run to run, so treat differences
under about 5 % as noise. Compare one change at a time: no switch, `-VCacheCcd`,
`-NoPatches`. The log says how many regions patch 1 put in place and how many slots patch 2
missed. If patch 2 keeps reporting missed slots, leave it out.
